> **All results on this page are row-level:** recordings of the same person can appear in both the training and the test set, which inflates scores. Subject-wise results are reported separately (M2).

## Summary

The paper reports **87.18%** SVM test accuracy (34 of 39 test recordings correct) from one stratified 80/20 split. The original notebook and its random seed are not available, so the exact split cannot be recovered. Over 200 random stratified 80/20 splits, the same SVM setup scores **87.86% ± 3.58%** (5th–95th percentile 82.05% – 92.31%); 24.00% of splits give exactly 87.18% and 68.50% give at least that. The paper's figure is therefore consistent with this setup, but a single split cannot pin a model's accuracy down to better than several percentage points. Always predicting "Parkinson's" scores 74.36% on the seed-42 test split.

With default settings, the best model by 10-fold row-level CV accuracy is **Gradient Boosting** (93.37% ± 5.51%), against SVM's 87.66% ± 6.94%. SVM's high accuracy hides weak performance on the healthy group: its specificity is 51.50% ± 26.65% and its balanced accuracy 75.42% ± 13.75%, because about 75% of recordings are Parkinson's.

## Matching the paper's protocol

The paper (Section III) scales the features, makes a stratified 80/20 split, and tunes an RBF SVM's C and gamma by grid search with 5-fold cross-validation; it reports **88.46% training** and **87.18% test** accuracy. The grid itself is not given; the one used here was fixed before running: `C` ∈ [0.1, 1, 10, 100], `gamma` ∈ ['scale', 0.01, 0.1, 1].

| Setup | Training accuracy | Test accuracy | Splits with test = paper | Splits with both = paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper (Table I) | 88.46% | 87.18% | – | – |
| SVM, default settings, 200 splits | 89.87% ± 1.13% | 87.86% ± 3.58% | 48 / 200 | 3 / 200 |
| SVM, grid search (as described), 100 splits | 99.84% ± 0.55% | 93.21% ± 2.98% | 8 / 100 | 0 / 100 |

The reported pair is consistent with an SVM with **default settings**: its average training and test accuracy are close to the paper's, and some splits reproduce both numbers exactly. With the grid search the paper describes, the same protocol fits the training data almost perfectly and scores higher on the test set, so the reported figures most likely come from an untuned model. Choosing one of the matching splits and calling it "the paper's split" would be selecting a seed after seeing the results, so this report does not do that.
