## Summary

The paper reports **87.18%** SVM accuracy (34 of 39 test recordings correct, i.e. one 80/20 split). The original notebook and its random seed are not available, so the exact split cannot be recovered. Over 200 random 80/20 splits, the same SVM setup scores **87.74% ± 4.88%** (5th–95th percentile 79.49% – 94.87%); 17.50% of splits give exactly 87.18% and 64.50% give at least that. The paper's figure is therefore consistent with this setup, but a single split cannot pin a model's accuracy down to better than several percentage points. Always predicting "Parkinson's" scores 82.05% on the seed-42 test split.

With default settings, the best model by 10-fold row-level CV accuracy is **Gradient Boosting** (93.37% ± 5.51%), against SVM's 87.66% ± 6.94%. SVM's high accuracy hides weak performance on the healthy group: its specificity is 51.50% ± 26.65% and its balanced accuracy 75.42% ± 13.75%, because about 75% of recordings are Parkinson's.
